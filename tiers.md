# AI Humanization Prompt Matrix

## [Tier 1] Light Polishing Instructions
Reduce common AI telltales while maintaining formal structure. Focus on precision and flow without inducing conversational cues.

1. Replace complex connecting words ("Furthermore," "Consequently," "In light of") with simpler alternates.
2. Eliminate all "idealized" descriptions (e.g., "a unique tapestry of," "in today's fast-paced world").
3. Adjust sentence length to avoid repetitive structures without leaning on ultra-short fragments as a default.
4. Keep tone professional but identically human-edited.


## [Tier 2] Natural Rewriting Instructions
Implement a conversational and fluent narrative flow that mimics human speech patterns.

1. Vary sentence length so no run of sentences reads metronomically; favor a moderate band and keep at least a third of sentences comfortably long.
2. Casual Transitions: Use "But," "So," or "And" to start sentences or connect ideas where appropriate... but don't overdo it.
3. Ditch the Robots: Obliterate phrases like "It is important to contemplate the implications of..."
4. Control the Confidence: Lower the absolute tone. Use words like "perhaps," "maybe," "might," and "in some cases" to sound more cautious and less algorithmic.


## [Tier 3] Expressive Voice
Break the typical "AI voice" which is neutral and equidistant from all sides. Give the world an individual viewpoint.

1. Punch Up Verbs: Replace passive voice with active, aggressive, or flavorful verbs.
2. Rhetorical/Emotional Cues: Use occasional interjections or address the reader directly to create an immediate connection.
3. Recombine and Reshape: Split and merge clauses, and vary how paragraphs open and close, so the structure feels hand-built rather than template-shaped.
4. Bookend Frame: Consider opening and closing the piece with an echoed image or motif.
5. Nuance Injection: Find the one fact that feels dry and expand it into a vivid description of its reality.


## Global Constraints Across All Tiers
- Zero Fabrication: Do not invent examples or quotes.
- Meaning Preservation: If the original says "The system failed because x," the result must say exactly that, just more humanly.
- Formatting Preservations: If the user has citations or bolding, keep them.
- Fragments in Moderation: One- or two-word sentences are a legitimate device; use them deliberately, not as a default filler. If they recur more than a couple of times per page, merge the weakest into fuller clauses.
- Structure Serves the Writing: Prefer sentence- and paragraph-level rework (clause shape, openings, closings) over reordering whole sections, which rarely improves a text.
- Distinctive Over Neutral: Prefer confident, characterful phrasing; overly "cleaned-up" prose can read as generic.
